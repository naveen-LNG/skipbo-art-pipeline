~~~~text
Reproduce the hand-drawn pencil drawing style of the attached style reference images as exactly as possible. Those reference images define the line quality, the line weight relationships between subject and background, the level of detail, the paper, and the overall feel of the drawing. The subject and the scene described are new, so take the style from the references and nothing else from them: not their subjects, not their poses, not their compositions.

MEDIUM: A hand-drawn pencil sketch on smooth bristol paper in a soft near-white that carries only the faintest hint of warmth, not cream, not ivory and definitely not yellow, drawn in HB graphite. Monochrome, one pencil color only, with even and fairly light hand pressure throughout. The graphite reads as a neutral pencil grey with the barest trace of warmth in it, never brown and never yellow. It looks like a real drawing made by a hand holding a pencil and photographed flat, not like a digital illustration.

LINE: The drawing is built from confident single-pass contour lines with a slight hand-drawn wobble, and the grain of the pencil is visible in every stroke. Lines overshoot a little where they cross. Every stroke tapers, thin where it begins and where it ends, carrying weight only through its middle or at the deepest point of a form, so no line ever holds a constant thickness. Many contours simply fade out mid-stroke and leave the eye to close the shape, so the outlines are open and broken rather than sealed, and edges are lost wherever a form turns away from the light. Every line in the image is drawn freehand, and this includes every line in the background. Nothing is ruled and nothing is perfectly straight. Long edges waver slightly, horizons are not dead level, verticals lean a little off true, corners do not meet exactly, and the perspective is judged by eye rather than measured, so the scene carries the gentle imperfection of a hand-drawn sketch instead of the crispness of a technical drawing.

LINE WEIGHT: The whole drawing is made with a single HB pencil at a light, even hand, and the darkest mark anywhere on the page is a light to mid grey graphite, never black, never inked and never dense. The hierarchy is clear and must be kept: the main subject carries the strongest and most confident line on the page and is immediately, unmistakably the focus of the drawing. The main subject's contour is drawn with real conviction, in firm and decisive strokes with clear variation of weight along their length, the kind of line an experienced hand lays down in a single pass without hesitating. That contour is never thin, timid, uniform, wispy or tentative. What changes is only the size of the gap between the subject and everything else. The background and the secondary elements are drawn at roughly three quarters of the main subject's pencil pressure, close enough that they clearly belong to the same drawing made by the same hand, and far enough apart that the subject still plainly leads. Bringing the background closer in line weight does not mean drawing more of it: only the darkness of the lines moves closer together, while the number of lines stays extremely low everywhere on the page. Nothing in the scene is reduced to a faint ghost or a pale afterthought. If you squint at the finished drawing, the main subject still reads at once as the focus, and yet no part of the background disappears. The background holds its full weight right up to where it meets the main subject, on every side of it equally, on its left exactly as much as on its right, with no pale halo, no soft gap, no washed-out zone and no empty breathing space cleared anywhere around it.

FORM AND SHADING: The forms are appealing cartoon construction, clean simple volumes, a clear readable silhouette, expressive rounded shapes. There is almost no shading anywhere. A few loose parallel hatch strokes describe the ground contact shadow, and occasionally three or four strokes sit inside a deep fold, and nothing more than that. Large areas of the paper are left completely untouched, and that bare paper is part of the drawing.

DETAIL ECONOMY: This is a quick, clean sketch and not a finished rendering, so the entire page carries as few lines as possible. If a line can be left out and the drawing still reads clearly, it is left out. The main subject is drawn almost entirely in clean contour: the silhouette, the features of the face, the hands or feet, and only a handful of the most important folds or forms. There is no hatching, no shading, no texture and no stippling anywhere on the main subject, and no construction lines are left on its face. The background is a bare suggestion of a place rather than a described scene, and there are no repeated small units of any kind anywhere in the image, which rules out brick courses, stone blocks, glazing bars, fence pickets, roof tiles, individual leaves, repeated ripples, fabric weave, panel seams and every similar pattern. Any secondary or distant element is a simple open outline of three to five lines, never filled in with grey tone, and distant figures carry no faces, no fingers and no clothing detail.

AVOID: Any drawn border, frame, ruled rectangle or box enclosing the image, since the paper simply continues past the drawing on all four sides. Ruled, mechanical or perfectly straight lines, and any background that looks like a technical or CAD drawing. A pale halo of empty paper cleared around the main subject. A cold, blue-grey or clinical looking page, and equally any yellow or cream cast. Hatching, shading, texture or stippling on the main subject. Repeated small units or pattern anywhere in the image. Secondary elements filled in with grey tone. A harsh jump in line darkness between the main subject and the scene around it. A background reduced to a pale ghost behind the subject. A dark, near-black or inked outline on the main subject. Bold or uniform outlines, and anything that looks drawn in pen or marker. Smooth vector or anti-aliased digital lines. Smudged or blended graphite, heavy crosshatching, dramatic contrast. Color, ink washes, halftone or cel shading. A background rendered as fully as the subject. Watermarks, signatures, captions and titles.
~~~~
