Where the regressions come from

1. Negative instructions cannot work at all under these settings

Every one of the 429 generations ran cfg 1.0 with ConditioningZeroOut wired into the sampler's negative input. At CFG 1 there is no classifier-free guidance, so the negative branch is mathematically ignored — and it was zeroed out anyway. Every no X / NOT X phrase is being fed to the model as positive conditioning.

That is why the same prohibitions keep reappearing: writing “not pink, not a pig snout” puts pink and pig snout into the positive prompt. The corrections re-summon the thing they forbid.

Evidence: 429/429 jobs — cfg 1.0, 8 steps, res_multistep, negative = ConditioningZeroOut. Most-repeated prohibition phrases across the prompt corpus:

2. Screenshot-only feedback drives three quarters of the burn

18 of the 26 triggering turns were an annotated screenshot with no words at all. They produced 321 of the 429 generations at a mean of 17.8 per turn, against 13.5 for turns that carried a sentence. The two most expensive turns of the whole session — 79 generations / 150 GPU-min, and 39 generations / 112 GPU-min — were both wordless.

With no words, the fault has to be inferred from the picture, so several candidate fixes get generated in parallel instead of one targeted fix.

Evidence: turns 3, 5, 6, 8–12, 14, 16–20, 22–24 and 26 in the table above.

3. Seed roulette substitutes for fixing the prompt

197 generations — 46% of them, 6 GPU-hours — reran a byte-identical prompt on a new seed. Only 83 generations involved actually rewriting the prompt. When a result is wrong for a structural reason (wrong snout, arm fused to the vest), re-rolling cannot fix it, and the session re-rolled roughly 2.4 times for every prompt rewrite.

Evidence: 429 generations across 232 distinct prompt texts; 149 prompt clusters.

4. A handful of targets get reopened across many separate turns

28 of the 149 prompt clusters span more than one of your turns, absorbing 141 generations. The worst is the Moose's face, reopened in five separate turns.

What to change

Rewrite prohibitions as positive descriptions

Replace every no X with the thing you do want in that slot: not “big dark-brown nose (not pink, not a pig snout)” but “a broad dark-brown nose leather with two wide nostrils at the tip of a long tan muzzle”. Or, if negatives are genuinely needed, raise CFG above 1 and feed a real second CLIPTextEncode instead of ConditioningZeroOut — but that changes Z-Image Turbo's tuned operating point, so the positive rewrite is the cheaper move.

Targets the 36 repeats each of “no human skin / no human nose / no mustache” and the 135 of “no text”.

Cap re-rolls at two, then force a prompt change

A standing rule: after two seeds on the same prompt, the next job must carry different words. Half of the 197 re-rolls were third-or-later attempts on identical text.

Even a 50% cut here saves roughly 3 GPU-hours per session of this size.

Put one sentence on every screenshot

The screenshot shows where; one line says what — “snout too short”, “arm fused to vest”, “this one is fine, fix only the eyes”. Worded turns already run 24% leaner. Saying what is acceptable matters as much as what is wrong: it stops a cluster being reopened a fifth time.

Against 321 generations currently driven by wordless turns.

Settle style against a reference before generating a cast

The Blocks-match constraint that ended the search in turn 4 could have been given in turn 1. Pin an accepted frame as the style reference and run img2img from it rather than exploring style with text-to-image.

Against the 91 generations and 142 GPU-minutes spent on turns 1–4.

Review in one batch, not one asset at a time

Clusters reopened across three to five turns are the expensive ones. Generating a contact sheet of every part and approving it in a single pass collapses those turns into one.

Against the 141 generations sitting in multi-turn clusters.

Method and limits

  • Join key is time: each job's execution_start falls between two consecutive user messages in session 98e97613, whose transcript spans 8–11 Oct exactly. 603/603 jobs land inside a gap, and none precede the first message, so attribution is unambiguous — but it is attribution by adjacency, not by a recorded causal link.
  • Only 57 of the session's 114 user records are genuine messages from you; the rest are task notifications, system reminders and command echoes, and were excluded.
  • “Prompt cluster” uses a difflib ratio of 0.80 on the first 600 characters. A looser threshold would show more rework, a tighter one less.
  • GPU minutes are server-side execution time per job, not queue or wall-clock time, and they include jobs queued back to back.
  • This covers one session's window. The August and September work is gone from the server's history, so these ratios are not proven to generalise beyond character-rig work.
