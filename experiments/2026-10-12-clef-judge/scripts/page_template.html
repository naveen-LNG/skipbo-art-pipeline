Clef Rig Judge Trial

Can a fast vision decision model look at a rendered rig frame and tell us whether the parts are stitched correctly, which part is wrong, and which way to nudge it? We tested Cloudflare's Clef Flash (9B) on renders of the approved fox and moose rigs, with defects we planted, and on the defects the human reviewer actually caught.

Spot a stitching defect in one frame?No

P(defect) stays near 0.2 for clean frames and for parts shifted 24 px. No threshold separates them.

Say which part, and which way to move it?No

Part and direction answers are at chance, even on joint crops with the master as reference.

Tell a rejected full-body frame from its fix?Promising

Jev 1.14 separates them one frame at a time (AUROC 0.99 on 11 + 11), and 11 of 11 as pairs. Two characters, one review: needs more rounds.

How a call works

One forward pass, typed answers

Clef is a System One model. It does not write text. Code sends a state (here: a description of the rig plus the rendered image) and a set of typed questions. The model returns a probability for every allowed answer, and our code decides what to do with them.

RenderPipeline renders a frame from the rig (spine_qa renderer)~520×720 px PNG
RequestState text, the image as a data URI, 6 questions~1,500 input tokens
Clef FlashScores every option of every question in one passmedian {{LATENCY}}
Code decidesThresholds the probabilities: pass, flag, or nudge a partno parsing, no prose

Round 2 · 13 Oct · joint close-ups with the master as reference

Same joint, cropped from the master and from the render

Setup pose only, since that's the pose the master is drawn in. Each crop is a square centred on the joint the rig already knows (the bone's attachment point). The same square is mapped onto the master through report.json's master frame. 363 crops: 180 with one part shifted 8, 16 or 24 px in each direction, 183 untouched joints. Both conditions ran on the identical crops with the same two questions; only the master image differs.

What changed. Centring the crop on the joint, rendered at twice the resolution and in the setup pose only, lifted detection from 0.52 (round 1 crops) to {{R2_NO_AUC}}, and it now rises with the shift (0.58, 0.66, 0.71 for 8, 16, 24 px). This is the first sign of a real response.

What it sees. Visible gaps: a boot sliding out of its trouser leg, a hand dropping out of its cuff. A part pushed into its neighbour hides the seam and scores like a clean joint. Head, hoof and antler shifts barely register.

The master didn't help. The clean rig already differs from the master (narrowed hips, redrawn hands and tail), so "different from the master" fires on clean joints too. With the master, tail answers are a flat 0.32 whatever happened.

Direction stays at chance: Flash answers "up" for most shifts. Crude code registration already does better ({{R2_REG_D}}), and aligning the moved part's own mask would do better still.

Direction is still at chance. Flash answered "up" for most shifts; 27B answers "down".

Comparison got more confident. Asked which of two renders of the same pose is worse, 27B is surer on the cases Flash already got right. That's the one task where model size helps.

Round 4 · 15 Oct · Jev 1.14 from TypeSafe, with the new Box question

Jev reads the real rejected frames, and Box finds parts but not offsets

The same images and the same question wording, sent to jev-1.14.0 with images as assets referenced from the state. Jev 1.14 adds a Box question: name a target, get back its four edges as fractions of the image, plus a confidence. We asked Box for the moved part in the joint crops (in both the master crop and the render), and for "the part that looks wrong" in full frames. 982 calls, 1.75M tokens, about $0.07, median 0.5 s per call.

{{R4_ROWS}}
Detection (AUROC) on identical itemsClef FlashClef 27BJev 1.14

The real rejected frames. Judged one frame at a time, Jev separates the 11 full-body frames the reviewer rejected from their 11 approved fixes almost perfectly (AUROC 0.99, 95% CI 0.94–1.00). Rejected frames scored {{R4_REAL_BF}}; approved fixes {{R4_REAL_OK}}. Clef managed 0.73–0.76. Sleeve-over-torso is still uncertain, and it's two characters from one review sheet, so this needs more real rounds before it gates anything.

Planted pixel shifts are still mostly invisible to it in full frames (0.45). On joint crops it is level with Clef once it sees the master (0.65).

{{R4_BOXES}}

Finding the part works roughly. Mean IoU {{R4_MIOU}}; {{R4_HIT}} of boxes overlap the true part by half or more.

Reading the offset doesn't. Subtracting the master box from the render box gives the right fix direction on {{R4_DIRV}} of the {{R4_NV}} shifts a box could show. Perfect boxes get 100% on the same shifts. Jev's box edges are off by a median 9% of the crop, while an 8, 16 or 24 px shift moves an edge 6%, 11% or 17%. The box would need to be about twice as tight, or the crop much closer on the part.

With the master crop. Confidence is low everywhere (about 0.03–0.09) and doesn't rank the good boxes (rank correlation {{R4_RHO_R}}; {{R4_RHO_J}} with the render alone). As a probability it is far off (calibration error {{R4_ECE_R}}). Use it only after tuning a threshold on your own crops.

Box on full frames: "the part that looks wrong"

Across 82 defective frames, the box centre lands in the changed region {{R4_PT}} of the time. A random point lands there about 7% of the time, so it is three times chance and still too weak to point a fixer at a part.

These figures cover the Flash runs. The 27B rerun (Round 3) added 982 calls and about 0.98M tokens, about 21k Neurons or about $0.23 at list price. The local Ollama build (12 GB) would not load on the 16 GB Mac: Metal caps GPU memory at 11.8 GiB. All calls ran on Cloudflare Workers AI. One contract difference: Cloudflare wants images as data URIs; Ollama wants raw base64.

Open for discussion

Where we could take it next

Run the same set on Clef 27B

Tests whether model size is the limit. Same harness, one flag.

~6× Flash price · core set fits one day's free tier

Grow the pairwise check

Collect every fix round (round N vs N+1) from past sessions and see if 10/11 holds on 50+ pairs. This is the one signal that might earn a place in the loop.

~$0.00004 per pair, both orders

Gap check per joint, thresholds per joint

Round 2 shows Clef responds to visible gaps at hands and boots. Ask only "is there a gap between X and Y", and calibrate one threshold per joint type, since clean boots already score 0.64.

~700 tokens per joint · ~10 joints per frame

Round-to-round check on 27B

27B is the stronger comparer (17/19, full-body pairs at 0.76 mean). Collect real fix rounds from past sessions and test "is round N+1 better than round N" on 50+ pairs.

~2.2k tokens per pair, both orders ≈ 50 Neurons

Let code find offsets

For nudges, align each part's own mask to the master. Crude whole-crop registration already beats Clef on direction ({{R2_REG_D}} vs about 25%). Keep Clef for "does this read right" questions.
