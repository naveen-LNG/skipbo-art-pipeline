What Jev judges well, and what it doesn't

Bars show the measured score; the black tick is chance for that question (8-way within 45° = 0.375, 6-way pose = 0.17, 4-way direction = 0.25, AUROC and 2-way = 0.5).

Sources for the bars

  • Finger, thumb, pose, palm/back direct, left/right: fox + moose pieces, n = 304 · results/pieces/scores_text.json
  • Thumb up, mirrored: fox + moose rendered frames, n = 120 · results/holds/scores_text.json; beaver final thumb up 1.00 · holds_beaver2
  • Palm/back cues: results/pieces/scores_cues.json
  • Full-body frames, moved joint, pixel direction, planted frames: clef-judge rounds 4–5 README

Not on the chart because there is no usable number

  • Absolute joint judgement. Every approved fox and moose joint scores P(matches master) < 0.5. Only comparisons carry signal.
  • Whole-frame defect on a finished rig. Final beaver mean P(defect) 0.559 vs approved 0.557. The ranked list was hold_card on every run, a known art issue.
  • Box. IoU 0.50, confidence miscalibrated (ECE 0.37–0.48), edges too coarse for 8–24 px shifts.
  • Held-out drop. On the beaver, finger 80% and pose 69%, against 96% and 91% on the characters the rules were tuned on.

Assessment

Where Jev should not be the decider

  • Palm vs back, and which hand. Use the annotation and the anatomy rule in code. Cue scores can raise a question; they can't settle one. Three beaver palms sat within 0.011 of the cut-off.
  • Any fix size or direction. 34% on 4-way nudges. Keep colour measurements, mask alignment and sweeps in code.
  • Any absolute threshold on a joint or frame score. Compare against approved rigs and the previous build instead.
  • Approval of a build. Frames and joints are reading lists. The one gate worth keeping is thumb up on point and thumbs-up holds.
  • Overruling the aligned crops. When Jev and a person disagree (the wrists), the person decides by looking.

Claims in the README that the code does not support

Call #2 asked no palm/back question. jev_annotate.py does ask facing, with examples. It never got past P(palm) 0.51, so it could not raise a doubt.
Check --jev would have caught the cheer fist (also in spine-parts.md). The fist was annotated back, correctly, and Jev also says back. facing_doubt fires only on disagreement, so no doubt is raised. The defect was a back-facing fist in a raised pose, and no rule checks that.
Jev QA found the wrist and ankle regressions. Pairs rated the ankles better in every run. They are only above the approved max, and that WARN was never cleared.

Gaps: defects no check is aimed at

1

Belt and waist in motion. Joint crops are rendered in the setup pose only. The only Jev look at animation is a whole-frame noul at 720 px, which scores the final beaver the same as the approved rigs (0.559 vs 0.557). spine_qa's stitched and hole checks are geometric and passed. A waist gap at cheer apex or mid-jump has no targeted check.

Run joint_crops at animation extremes, not just the setup pose. Ask Jev pairwise against the same joint's setup-pose crop; it detects "moved" at 91%. In code, measure the gap pixels between sweater hem and belt per frame.

2

Boots beside trouser cuffs. sockets measures spill past the parent's rim near one opening centre, not a boot showing beside a narrower cuff. Jev flagged both ankles above the approved max in all three QA runs. The final joints sheet shows a visible step at the left cuff. Nobody acted on the WARN.

In code, compare the boot top's width with the cuff opening's width, against the master's ratio, in every socket frame. Turn a persistent joint WARN into a checkpoint-6 doubt, with the crop shown.

3

Hand size vs cuff. Hands are sized by wrist-to-fingertip length and tuned with a sweep. Nothing compares mitten width with cuff width. Jev's wrist pairs preferred the earlier build (0.16 / 0.38) and were overruled. The final crops show a smaller mitten than the master's.

Add a code ratio: hand width over cuff opening, rig vs master, within ±10%. Then use Jev pairwise on the wrist crop to break ties.

4

Lower-body proportions vs the master. setup checks one global silhouette IoU (≥ 0.85), and landmarks checks joint points within 1%. Neither checks widths or lengths of trouser legs and boots. The trousers were repainted by script (make_trousers.py), and 15% of the silhouette can differ without a failure.

Run per-region IoU and width profiles against the master (hips band, each leg, each boot). Jev's joint Score is already above the approved max at the ankles and can rank which region to inspect first.

5

Hold check sees one hand and never palm/back. hold_crops takes one slot per hold, so cheer_jump's second fist is never looked at. Facing isn't asked at holds. A back-facing raised fist annotated correctly passes today's pipeline end to end.

Crop both hands per hold. Add a code rule per gesture on the annotated facing (a raised cheer fist shows its palm). Run the cue score on the rendered hold crop as a second opinion.

6

Thresholds tuned in-sample. The palm/back band was set on the fox and moose pieces it is scored on. Approved point holds sit at P(up) 0.66, only 0.16 above the FAIL line.

Re-check the band and the 0.5 thumb cut on the next new character before trusting them. Treat calls near a cut-off as "ask".
