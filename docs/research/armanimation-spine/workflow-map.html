How a Spine animator turns one character image into a smooth rig

These are the stages a professional Spine animator works through, from cut-out parts to the first idle loop. Each stage notes who should own it in our pipeline: our generator, the animator in Spine, or the feedback capture that turns the animator's fixes into data. Learnings are bucketed by character type so that humanoid rules don't leak into animal rigs.

Show rules for

Our beaver, fox and moose are biped bodies with animal heads, and the channel has no clean example of that. Use Cartoon humanoid for the body and limbs and Quadruped animal for the head, ears, eyes and mouth. Annotate them as a separate anthro-biped-animal bucket.

The workflow

  1. Plan the motion before cutting

    Generator
    • Ask the developers for the Spine version and whether meshes and constraints are allowed.
    • List the animations (idle, jump, talk) and "understand who your character is". Cut only what those animations need.
    animation listSpine version
    • QuadrupedForeground legs get the full rig. Background legs are simplified or copied from the foreground set.
    • Cartoon humanA small character talks with 3 swapped mouths. A large one needs a meshed mouth.

  2. Cut parts in Photoshop

    Generator
    • Cut one layer per independently moving part, with a unique name. That name becomes the bone, slot and attachment.
    • End joints round so a rotation never shows a seam. Don't over-cut: one mesh bends better than three pieces.
    • Deliver a straightened copy of curved parts (Puppet Warp or Liquify). Test joints by holding Alt and dragging the transform pivot.
    layer = bone nameround joint endsstraightened copy
    • QuadrupedCut eyes, upper and lower lids, lashes, pupils, nostrils, and mouth with chin as separate layers. Eyebrows stay in the head mesh.
    • RealisticStraighten the curved hair or scarf before rigging (Tracer).

    Splitting a flat master, from the most-viewed Live2D, Spine and After Effects separation tutorials:

    • Paint every part complete. Repaint the torso under the arms, the back hair under the face, and the ground behind a cut-out object. Moving a part must never reveal a hole.
    • Underfill: put a darker flat copy of each silhouette under it, so a gap reads as shadow.
    • Round joint caps: draw a circle on each pivot and merge it into both limb ends.
    • Split eyes into white, iris, highlight, upper and lower lash, lid and brow. Split mouths into lips, teeth, tongue and interior. Cut long hair into A/B/C segments.
    • For painterly art, cut with Select and Mask and rebuild hidden areas with the Clone Stamp.
    • BackgroundCut each element from the flat painting (Color Range for foliage), repaint what it covered, and reassemble the layers in Spine. Clouds ride a path.

  3. Import with tags

    Generator
    • Import the PSD with tags in the layer names. Pivots are never set in the PSD; the animator places them.
    [bone][slot][skin:name][mesh][mesh:source][merge][folder][ignore][origin][overlay]

  4. Build the skeleton

    Generator draftAnimator fixes pivots
    • Never animate root. The first bone is a character container, then pelvis at the centre of mass.
    • The pivot goes in the middle of the round joint piece. To find it, loop between two extreme poses and Alt-redraw the bone until the bend looks right.
    • Use as few bones as possible. No neck bone: a mesh bridges chest and head.
    container boneAlt-redrawCtrl+click = bone on image
    • QuadrupedBody splits into rotation-only hips and chest branches. The chest sits midway between the front legs. Tail is a 2–5 bone chain with a rigid tip. The shadow sits outside the container.
    • Cartoon humanChain: pelvis → hip → leg → foot, and pelvis → waist → chest → head. Make one 3-bone finger and fake the others.
    • RealisticPut the pelvis a little behind the body. One slot holds two leg attachments (standing vs contact). Digitigrade legs are a 3-bone chain plus foot (Dragonborn).
    • CreatureWing joints are siblings under the shoulder, each with a red control bone. Each feather row is its own bone chain.

  5. Add helper pivots

    AnimatorCapture
    • Add bones that hold no image of their own. They give the "extra layer" of motion that makes a rig feel soft.
    • Turn off inherit rotation on head and shoulders so they stay upright as the torso swings, and inherit scale on shoulders and neck so breathing scales only the chest.
    face (scale = 3D turn)back_head −100%ear chain ≤3eyelid curvechest2 bendchin / mouth-scale
    • QuadrupedHead rig: back_head, cheek_L/R, ear_L > ear_L1, eyebrows, whiskers (1 bone each), and eye with ball IK, lid and shine. Eyelid bones only curve the lid; the blink is a closed / happy-closed swap. Eyes sit in empty sockets, and both pupils are aimed by one shared target bone.
    • Cartoon humanExtra pelvis pivots (pelvis2/3/5/7) for shoulders and details. A big yellow chest2 helper is lightly weighted to bend the whole torso. The face bone took "20 seconds".
    • RealisticA face_3D bone drives the face. The skewed "ring" face rig takes about an hour, so use it for hero heads only.

  6. Mesh and weight what bends

    AnimatorCapture
    • Mesh only what bends. Props, eyes and highlights stay rigid images on a bone.
    • Put dense vertices on the inside of a bend and few elsewhere, with bones sitting on vertex rows. Avoid long thin triangles.
    • Weights: 100% on one bone away from a joint, with a narrow blend band at the joint. Paint one bone at a time with the others locked, then Smooth. Smooth only spreads along edges, so add manual edges as routes.
    meshmanual (orange) edgesbind + locksmooth / pruneavoid deform keys
    • QuadrupedThe body mesh binds to the controller bones, not the IK children. Eyebrows sit inside edge rings so they don't stretch the face.
    • Cartoon humanA leg can be a 5-vertex mesh. Heel weights split about 62/38 between foot and leg. Keep triangles from crossing the scar. Mouth: container bone plus a scale bone locked at 30%.
    • RealisticFace weights fall off from 100% at the centre to about 80% at the nose and 0–5% at the ears. Fingers have 3 outer and 2 inner vertices per bend.
    • CreatureA long raptor body has orange edges across it at every bone.

    This is our biggest gap. Every part we generate is a rigid image, 100% on one bone.

  7. Add constraints

    GeneratorAnimator tunes
    • Use IK only for external contact (ground, prop, hand on waist), and never on an arm just to steer it. 2-bone IK with Stretch; bend the legs slightly before creating it.
    • IK plus a transform constraint lets one control bone both move and rotate the foot.
    IK 2-bone + stretchtarget-to-foot transformtransform (follow / mirror)slider (4.3)
    • QuadrupedThe IK target is a child of the paw. Rear legs get a hand-moved "reversed bone" instead of a double IK. A transform constraint keeps the targets following when airborne. Ears follow the head through transform constraints.
    • Cartoon humanRedraw the foot so it touches the shin end, or stretching slides it. Parent the IK targets outside the leg, under the container. A hand on a cane gets arm IK because it touches something.
    • RealisticProp drives the hand: the hand is a child of the staff and the arm IK targets the hand.
    • CreatureWing: transform constraints at 50% make the middle bones follow halfway. Spider: additive, clamped 4.3 transforms make a procedural step. Turtle lips ride a path constraint.

  8. Organize for hand-off

    Generator
    • Colour controllers red with big icons. Put IK targets in a named selection and hide them; don't make them transparent.
    • Keep images inside bones and slots in folders.
    bone colour / iconselection groupsslot folders

  9. Animate at 80% rig

    AnimatorCapture rig edits
    • "Bring it to 80% and then jump into the animation": the rig keeps changing during animation.
    • Idle: a 60-frame breathing loop, offset limb rotations, then duplicated variations. Split the hip's X and Y timelines so paths curve.
    Ctrl+Shift+L key allghostingoffsetsseparate X/Y
    • QuadrupedJump: animate only the main bone's Y, then rotation. The face goes before the legs, and tail, ears and fur come last. Front legs lift first and land first.
    • Cartoon humanWeight-shift variation: the hip leads the foot by 8 frames. Key everything at both transition edges.

Where the extra pivots go

Schematics based on the cow, pig and rabbit rigs and the mutant brute rig. Grey bones carry images. Amber bones are helper pivots that exist only for smoother motion.

The feedback loop to capture

The generator emits a first-pass Spine project. The animator fixes it in Spine. Diffing the exported JSON against what we generated gives labelled corrections, tagged with the same bucket and stage as the learnings.
